# Wi-Fi: WLANs, roaming, steering & RF planning

Read this when working on SSIDs, security modes, 802.11r/k/v, steering, scans/surveys, the channel planner or floor plans.
Milestones: M2.6 (RF insight, read-only), M5.1 (WLANs, client kick/block), M5.2 (roaming, steering), M6.1 (planner, apply), M6.2 (heatmap).

## WLANs (SSIDs) [M5.1]

Fields (✅ in the model and the editor; D-083):
- ✅ SSID, enabled, network (VLAN), security, passphrase (vault)
- ✅ bands (2.4/5/6 GHz) and nodes (all | only | all but)
- ✅ hidden, client isolation, max clients per radio (`maxassoc`)
- ✅ schedule: weekly on-times in the site's time zone, daylight saving included. The controller enforces the saved site's (no apply needed): outside its times a WLAN's BSSes on managed nodes go off through the agent's `bss` op (hostapd's `config_set` with netifd's config less those BSSes; no UCI change, the radio's other BSSes and its mesh point keep running), and on again at the start; a `wifi reload` or an apply brings them back on, and the controller turns them off again within half a minute. While the controller is down a WLAN stays as it is. Events: `wlan_scheduled` (site-wide), `wlan_schedule_failed`/`wlan_schedule_ok` per node and phy (D-083).
- 802.11w (PMF) follows the security mode (transition 1, WPA3 and OWE 2)
- ✅ roaming (schema v7, D-085): fast roaming (802.11r) Auto, On or Off, and roaming hints (802.11k/v) on or off; see Roaming
- advanced, later: DTIM, multicast-to-unicast, `disassoc_low_ack`, minimum basic rate, airtime fairness

Security modes: WPA2-PSK, WPA2/WPA3 transition (`sae-mixed`), WPA3-SAE (required on 6 GHz), OWE for open guest networks, OWE in transition mode (an open companion for devices without OWE), plain open (warned about), and WPA2/3-Enterprise later. PPSK is open option O-8.

Rendering: one `wifi-iface` per WLAN × radio on every in-scope node, named `uw_<wlan>_<band>`, attached to the bridge of the WLAN's VLAN. SSID, key and mobility domain are identical across nodes. OWE in transition mode is two sections that name each other's interface (`owe_transition_ifname`): `uw_<wlan>_<band>o`, open under the WLAN's name on `uw<n>-open`, and `uw_<wlan>_<band>`, hidden as `<ssid>OWE` on `uw<n>-owe`; 6 GHz has no open BSS, so it gets the encrypted one alone.

UI: `/wifi` lists the WLANs (security, bands in the band colors, network, devices, schedule, the clients on each now, the document's warnings) and warns while the site has no country or a device doesn't manage `wireless`. Admins edit a WLAN in a dialog; a new one starts from a preset (Main: WPA2/WPA3 on 2.4 and 5 GHz; Guest: OWE in transition mode, isolated, on the guest network; IoT: WPA2 on 2.4 GHz, on the IoT network) and takes its id from its name. A QR code joins a phone: for a WLAN with a passphrase it is read from the vault (admins, audited). The site's name, country (the regulatory domain) and time zone are in Settings › Site.

Packages: `wpad-basic-mbedtls` has SAE, OWE, ACS, 802.11r and 802.11k on 24.10 and 25.12; 802.11v (BSS transition, WNM sleep), 802.11s mesh, EAP and WPS need the full `wpad-mbedtls` (the mesh also runs on `wpad-mesh-mbedtls`, which has 802.11v too). Each admin swaps it per device from its Config tab (D-085, D-089; see Roaming): the swap briefly drops that device's Wi-Fi.

## Client actions [M5.1]

✅ Admins kick and block clients from the client page (D-084). Neither changes configuration: the controller asks hostapd on every node that manages `wireless`, through the agent's `ban` op (hostapd's `del_client` on each BSS, with a ban time). Nodes in observe mode are never touched.

- **Kick:** deauthenticates the client wherever it is connected and keeps it off every managed access point for a time the admin picks (no time, 1 min (the default), 10 min, 1 hour; the API takes up to a day). The answer says where it was kicked from, that it wasn't connected to a managed access point, or that no device manages Wi-Fi. Kicks are kept in the controller's memory.
- **Block / unblock:** a block is kept with the client's record (`clients.blocked`, migration 0017; blocked clients are never pruned) and lasts until an admin lifts it. It belongs to the address alone: a device with private addresses may come back under a new one. The client page shows a Blocked badge with its time, the clients list marks blocked clients and filters them.
- **Enforcement** (`uniwrt_engine::ClientBans`): hostapd keeps bans in memory and its timer overflows past about 35 minutes, so the controller sends bans of at most 10 minutes and renews each node's bans within 5 minutes of lapsing (every 30 s it compares). A node that goes offline, a job's end (an apply's `wifi reload` restarts BSSes) and a banned client seen connected to a managed node send every ban again at once (at most every 10 s). A lift is a ban of 0, which skips the BSS the client is connected to (`del_client` would kick it). While the controller is down, bans lapse within 10 minutes.
- **Events:** `client_kicked` (with the ban and the user), `client_blocked`, `client_unblocked`; `client_bans_failed` raises `node:<id>/client-bans` when a node refuses, cleared by `client_bans_ok`. Kicks, blocks and unblocks are audited (`kick_client`, `block_client`).
- **API:** `POST v1/clients/{mac}/kick` (`ClientKick`: `ban_secs`, answered with a `KickOutcome`), `PUT`/`DELETE v1/clients/{mac}/block`.

## Roaming [M5.2]

Split (D-085): roaming a ✅ (render, keys, the editor, the lab's FT roam) | roaming b1 ✅ (controller-pushed neighbor reports) | roaming b2a ✅ (client capabilities, D-088) | roaming b2b ✅ (the per-device wpad swap, D-089) | steering.

- ✅ **Per WLAN** (`Wlan.roaming`, schema v7): `fast` is 802.11r, `null` (Auto) following the security mode: on for WPA2-PSK, **off for WPA3 and transition**, because OpenWrt 25.12's release notes list clients that fail 802.11r with WPA3; the admin can force it on (validation warns) or off. Modes without a passphrase never fast-roam. `hints` (on by default) is 802.11k and 802.11v. The editor has a Roaming section: Auto/On/Off with what Auto means for the mode, and a Roaming hints checkbox.
- ✅ **802.11r (Fast Transition)**, over the air (`ft_over_ds 0`, the most compatible), in a mobility domain of 4 hex digits from the WLAN's id (FNV-1a folded to 16 bits: the same on every node, kept when the SSID is renamed). WPA2-PSK generates its keys locally (`ft_psk_generate_local 1`). SAE and the transition mode hand the keys between access points (`ft_psk_generate_local 0`, wildcard `r0kh`/`r1kh`, so the APs learn each other over the network's bridge) under the WLAN's **FT key**: HMAC-SHA256 of the controller's roaming key (32 random bytes in the vault, `controller/roaming_key`, made the first time a WLAN fast-roams) over the WLAN's id and its passphrase's vault name. A new passphrase brings a new key, and clients that know the passphrase can't derive it (OpenWrt's default derives it from the passphrase). The key holders are secret options (masked on review screens). A node without 802.11r renders the WLAN without it, with a warning.
- ✅ **802.11k:** `ieee80211k 1` wherever hostapd has it (every OpenWrt hostapd since 23.05, the basic wpad's too).
- ✅ **Neighbor reports** (roaming b; the engine's `NeighborReports`, the agent's `rrm` op): the controller learns each managed node's BSSes with 802.11k (their own reports, `rrm_nr_get_own`) and gives each its neighbors with `rrm_nr_set`: the other BSSes of its SSID on the managed nodes, its own node's first (its other bands), then those of the nodes it hears best in the RF report's matrix (on the neighbor's band, else any), at most 16; a BSS without neighbors gets an empty list. No config churn: hostapd keeps lists in memory, so every node gets its lists every minute and the agent sets only those `rrm_nr_list` doesn't hold already; new own reports (a BSS restarted on another channel), a job's end, a node coming or going and a new matrix resend what changed at once. Observe-mode nodes are neither asked nor listed. A node that refuses raises `node:<id>/neighbor-reports` (`neighbor_reports_failed`, Wi-Fi warning), cleared by `neighbor_reports_ok`; a BSS gone meanwhile isn't a refusal. Lab: a's and b's 5 GHz access points take each other on both releases, and the same lists sent again change nothing.
- ✅ **802.11v:** `bss_transition 1`, `wnm_sleep_mode 1` and `wnm_sleep_mode_no_keys 1` (no keys in WNM sleep responses, for stations that would reinstall them), only where the agent found WNM in hostapd (the `11v` capability): netifd writes the options whatever hostapd was built with, and a hostapd without WNM refuses the whole BSS. A node without it warns once per WLAN (install the full wpad).
- ✅ **Import** reads `ieee80211r` into `fast` (unset where it matches the mode's default) and `ieee80211k`/`bss_transition` into `hints`; one access point with roaming brings it to the whole WLAN, so roaming never splits one. A node's own `r0kh`/`r1kh` are dropped: the controller's keys replace them (the import's residual shows them, masked).
- ✅ **Lab:** the system test fast-roams client c's 5 GHz uplink with FT-SAE from b (24.10) to a (25.12) and back on the rendered config; each AP's hostapd reports `sta-authorized` with `auth-alg` `ft`.
- ✅ **The full wpad, per device** (D-085, D-089): a device whose hostapd lacks 802.11v (the agent's `11v` capability; the basic wpad, as OpenWrt's images ship it) shows Basic wpad in its Config tab, and `/wifi` names such devices while a WLAN asks for roaming hints, each linked there. On a managed device an admin installs the full wpad (`POST /api/v1/nodes/{id}/wpad`, audited), a job: **check** (no open transaction; the wpad installed, `wpad-basic-<tls>` or `wpad-mesh-<tls>`, becomes `wpad-<tls>`; split hostapd packages, several wpads or an old variant are refused; 1.5 MiB of free flash and 8 MiB in `/tmp`) → **fetch** (the full wpad, a copy of the current one and `hostapd-common` into `/tmp/uniwrt-wpad`; apk under its cache's names, so `--no-network` trusts them through the index, then `apk add --simulate`; opkg's `download`; nothing changes, and a failure removes them) → **swap** (one script, detached with `setsid` and hangups ignored, because removing a wpad stops hostapd and `wpa_supplicant` and a mesh AP loses its link: `apk add --no-network --cache-dir … wpad-<tls> '!<old>'`, or `opkg remove` then `opkg install --cache …`; if neither wpad is left, it reinstalls the old one from its copy; it waits up to 30 s for every access point and mesh point to come back, else runs `wifi up`; it writes `swapped`, `unchanged`, `restored` or `broken`; the job logs in again as often as needed, up to 4 min, to read it) → **verify** (the agent finds 802.11v; the directory goes). The configuration doesn't change: the next apply renders 802.11v where WLANs ask for it, and the engine collects the device's facts again when the job ends. Lab (both releases): the basic wpad in place of the lab's full one, then the swap with the full package corrupted (the old wpad runs at the end, Wi-Fi back) and for real, offline once fetched (802.11v, every access point and mesh point back). The mesh wpad has 802.11v (checked on 25.12), so meshed devices rarely need the swap; the demo's factory gateway (`--demo-factory`) runs the basic one.
- ✅ **Client capabilities** (D-088): each Wi-Fi client's newest standard (its Wi-Fi generation), the bands it connected on, and its features (802.11k, 802.11v, 802.11w and WMM from hostapd's view of each association; 802.11r once an authorization says `auth-alg` `ft`, since hostapd names no AKM per station) are learned over its associations and kept on its record (the store's `clients.wifi`), only ever growing. The client page shows them under Details, while it's away too; steering uses them.

## Steering & balancing [M5.2]

Split (D-090, D-091): a1 ✅ (schema v8, render, import, the neighbor-list handover, the lab) | a2 ✅ (the UI: the site's profile on `/wifi`, the editor's opt-out) | b1 ✅ (the timeline's events) | b2 ✅ (the timeline in the client and device pages, D-092) | c1 ✅ (the `btm` op, D-093) | c2a ✅ (the engine's decisions, the render, the wording) | c2b ✅ (the sim).

- ✅ **Phase 1 (D-090, the owner's O-2):** usteer on the access points, set by **one profile for the site** (`SiteConfig.steering.profile`, schema v8): **Off** (the default: nothing changes until an admin picks one), **Conservative**, **Balanced**, **Aggressive**. Each WLAN takes part unless it opts out (`roaming.steer`); usteer tells WLANs apart only by SSID (`ssid_list`), so validation warns when two WLANs of one SSID differ, and when a profile has no WLAN to steer.
- ✅ **Rendering** (`crates/render/src/usteer.rs`): usteer runs where the profile isn't Off and the node carries a steered WLAN (on, in scope, on a band it has a radio of): the `usteer` package (one anonymous section: `network` the management network, `syslog`, `debug_level 1`, the profile's thresholds, `ssid_list`), the feed package installed by the apply (D-076) and its init script on. Nodes that manage `wireless` manage `usteer` (the v8 migration adds it), so a node that holds the package with steering off keeps its network settings and the service goes off. Every threshold is written, since 24.10's usteer (2022.08) and 25.12's (2025.10) default differently:

  | | Conservative | Balanced | Aggressive |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Band steering (802.11v clients, every / above) | 2 min / -60 dBm | 1 min / -63 dBm | 30 s / -66 dBm |
  | Roam trigger below | off | -75 dBm | -72 dBm |
  | Target AP stronger by | — | 8 dB | 5 dB |
  | Minimum signal (refuse, drop after 10 s) | off | off | -85 dBm |
  | Association steering, load kick | off | off | on, at 75 % channel load |

  Band steering and roam triggers are the controller's (c2a, D-093): usteer's own are rendered off (`band_steering_interval`, `roam_scan_snr`, `roam_trigger_snr` 0), and usteer keeps refusals, the minimum signal and load kicks (Aggressive), and `signal_diff_threshold` as association steering's margin (import reads it as Balanced). After a roam suggestion hostapd disconnects the client 10 s later unless it moved, so it reconnects to the better AP; band steering's requests are never followed by a disconnect. (Both releases ship usteer 2022.08: 25.12's 2025.10.04~1d6524c6 differs only in its build; its `aggressiveness` option exists only on usteer's later branch.) Without 802.11v on a node, render warns: Conservative does nothing there, the others can only disconnect.
- ✅ **hostapd refuses usteer's transition requests on 24.10 and 25.12**, so the controller sends them (c2a) (D-091, found in the hwsim lab): their `bss_transition_request` leaves `mbo_reason`, `cell_pref` and `reassoc_delay` uninitialized when the caller omits them, and with MBO compiled in (the full wpad) the range checks fail: ubus answers "Invalid argument" and nothing is sent. usteer 2022.08 omits them (its later branch sends them; OpenWrt main initializes them). The lab test fails once a release takes a request without the fields. The controller sends the requests itself (steering c, the owner's choice), with the fields.
- ✅ **Neighbor lists:** usteer sets the 802.11k lists of the SSIDs it steers every second (by roam history; nothing turns it off), so the controller's neighbor reports (D-086) leave those BSSes to it (`SiteConfig::steers`) and keep serving the others.
- ✅ **Import:** a node holding usteer brings the profile its options behave like (kicks or refusals → Aggressive, roam triggers → Balanced, else Conservative) and the WLANs its `ssid_list` names.
- ✅ **Lab:** a (25.12) and b (24.10) install usteer, run the rendered Balanced package (`get_config` reads back the thresholds and `ssid_list`), steer the lab SSID (`local_info`) and hear each other (`remote_info`); usteer is removed again.
- ✅ **The UI** (a2): `/wifi` has a Steering card: the four profiles as radio cards, each with what it does (the table above in words), and a note that ignored requests end in a disconnect. An admin picks one and saves it as one revision (`Steering: <profile>`); viewers see it read-only. While a choice is unsaved the card warns live when no WLAN takes part; otherwise it shows validation's `steering` warnings. With steering on, the card names the devices running the basic wpad (no 802.11v: Conservative does nothing there, the others can only disconnect), each linked to its Config tab, and the page's roaming-hints note gives way to it. The WLAN editor's Roaming section has **Steer clients** (`roaming.steer`), its hint naming the site's profile; the `wlans/<id>/roaming` warning (two WLANs of one SSID that differ, which only an import can bring, since the editor refuses a duplicate SSID) shows on the WLAN's row.
- ✅ **Timeline events** (b1, D-091): the agent's `usteer` topic subscribes to usteer's object, which notifies only while it has subscribers, for every event type (`event_log_types` filters only its syslog lines). The agent drops the accepts (every probe and association, and the roam state changes usteer mislabels `probe_req_accept`) and the load kick's steps that concern no client; it forwards `probe_req_deny`, `auth_req_deny` (never sent by 2022.08), `assoc_req_deny`, `signal_kick` and `load_kick_client`, with the reason (low signal, connect delay, a better candidate, repeated refusals), the client's signal, the threshold and the better AP (`<host>#hostapd.<ifname>`, or a sibling BSS). hostapd's `bss-transition-response` gives the client's answer and target BSSID; ucode reads its u8 status as a boolean, so only accepted or declined is known. A signal kick names no client: the engine takes it from the disconnect from the same AP that follows (within 2 s; usteer kicks right after notifying), else records it without one. The events log keeps each as `client_steered` (Client; Notice for kicks, Info otherwise) on the node and the client, with the AP steered to placed among the nodes (by the usteer's address or by BSSID) and the client's signal there; refusals of one client at one AP fold. The Events page names them ("Attic AP turned Pixel away · Signal -71 dBm · Better served by Gateway (-52 dBm)"), and the client and device pages link there. Lab (25.12): a transition request's answer, and a signal kick under a minimum above c's signal, pushed by the working tree's agent and parsed; c comes back.
- ✅ **Timeline UI** (b2, D-092): the client page's Timeline marks each steering event on its access point's lane, beside the roams (moved green, declined grey, turned away amber, disconnected red), and lists them under the lanes in words with a count of each outcome; an access point's device page has a **Steering** card when it steered anyone in the last 24 hours (counts, the newest, each linking to its client). Both read `GET v1/events?kinds=client_steered` each minute. A roam within one node (band steering) reads "switched access points". The sim steers where the controller asks (c2b): its `btm` op moves 802.11v clients, so the demo shows steering once a profile is applied to managed nodes (usteer installed and enabled, a wpad with 802.11v).
- **Controller-sent transition requests** (c, D-091, D-093; the owner's choice: the controller decides): so band steering and roam suggestions reach clients on 24.10 and 25.12, the engine makes those decisions on the profile's thresholds and sends the requests itself; usteer's band steering and roam triggers are turned off, and usteer keeps refusals, the minimum signal and load kicks. Requests pause while the controller is offline.
  - ✅ **The `btm` op** (c1): finds the access point the client is connected to (`not_found` elsewhere) and sends hostapd's `bss_transition_request` with the MBO fields (`mbo_reason`: low signal for roam triggers, premium AP for band steering; `cell_pref` 1; `reassoc_delay` only with imminent disassociation, at most 255 s), a dialog token, a validity of 100 beacon intervals, and the candidates best first, abridged (others not preferred); the controller gives each candidate its BSS's own report with a Candidate Preference subelement (`proto::ops::candidate`). `imminent` with `kick_ms` has hostapd disconnect the client after that long (its disassociation timer, in beacon intervals) unless it left. A hostapd without WNM answers `unsupported`. The client's answer is the `bss-transition-response` push. Lab (both releases): a request is sent and answered, and an imminent one's timer disconnects client c, which comes back.
  - ✅ **The decisions** (c2a, `crates/engine/src/steering/`): every 5 s, on each node where usteer runs, online and not held by a job, the authorized clients of the steered SSIDs with 802.11v (and a hostapd with it) are measured against the profile, each asked at most once per its band steering interval (2 min, 1 min, 30 s). **Band steering:** above the profile's signal on 2.4 GHz at every station sample through the interval, it is asked to the node's 5 and 6 GHz access points of its SSID (premium AP). **Roam suggestions** (Balanced, Aggressive): below the trigger, the node's usteer is asked where else it hears the client (`get_client_info`; its `connected` reaches the agent as a boolean that can't tell a left client, so the client's own access point is left out by the stations' facts); the access points of its SSID heard at least the margin stronger and not below the trigger are the candidates, best first, and it is asked with disassociation imminent (low signal; hostapd disconnects it after 10 s; no reassociation delay). Candidates carry their BSS's own 802.11k report (shared by the neighbor reports' task) with preferences 255, 254, …; an access point without 802.11k gives none. The client's answer is the `bss-transition-response` push (a `client_steered` event, as before); the request itself isn't recorded. Clients without 802.11v are no longer moved by usteer's roam triggers' disconnect (D-093: 802.11v clients only); Aggressive's refusals and kicks still move them. Lab: usteer's `get_client_info` on a places client c on the access point serving it, parsed as the engine reads it.
  - ✅ **The sim** (c2b): answers `btm` from the access point the client is on (`not_found` elsewhere, `unsupported` with the basic wpad): an 802.11v client (the sim's WPA3 ones) goes to the first candidate it can join (up, on a band it has, heard above -82 dBm), or up a band without candidates, after the accepted `bss-transition-response`; others ignore it. It answers usteer's `get_client_info` where usteer runs, from every steering node's access points that hear the client (above -90 dBm), and usteer no longer steers on its own. The opening walk's handover is now the client's own roam.
- **Phase 2 (optional):** controller-driven smart steering that uses global history (sticky-client detection) and sends BTM requests through hostapd ubus. Only clients that advertise 11v are steered, and steering simply stops while the controller is offline.

## RF insight (read-only) [M2.6]

`/insights/rf` (`features/rf`), the dashboard's RF card, and the pure `rf` crate behind them (D-064).

- **Scans** (`scan` op; `node-agent/scan.uc` in a process of its own, with its own nl80211 socket): one radio through its access point's interface (else its mesh point's), as an AP may scan (nl80211's AP and flush flags), over the band's channels its phy has; up to 20 s, aborted after. The answer carries each BSS's information elements (base64), which the controller parses (`crates/node/src/facts/ies.rs`: SSID, mesh ID, DS/HT/VHT/HE/EHT operation for the channel, width and center, the newest standard, RSN AKMs for the security, BSS load), and the phy's survey before and after the scan, whose difference is each channel's busy share and noise while it listened. Results the kernel cached from an earlier scan are left out.
- **Schedule** (D-064): background scans run each radio every 4 h (1 to 24, the `rf` setting; on by default), only while it is idle: its stations (mesh peers included) moved under 500 kbit/s in the two latest samples. A busy radio waits 10 minutes and tries again; a driver that cannot scan, 24 h. New radios are due 2 minutes after they appear, a minute apart. One scan runs at a time across the site, 30 s apart, on online nodes only. Admins queue scans of every radio, a node's or one at any time; those run first, busy or not. Scans are kept 30 days (`rf_scans`, migration 0012).
- **Report** (`uniwrt_rf::report`, rebuilt at most every 5 s and announced when it changes), per band:
  - *Radios:* channel, width, power, live busy share and noise, the foreign networks its node hears overlapping its channel, and its interference: the sum of their impacts, `weight(signal) × overlap`, where `weight` is a logistic curve from about nothing at −90 dBm to about everything at −60 dBm and `overlap` the share of the radio's channel the network covers. Under 0.3 is a clean channel, under 1 some overlap, else crowded.
  - *Channels* (each 20 MHz channel): allowed by any phy, DFS and its worst state, radar hits in 30 days (from `DFS-RADAR-DETECTED` lines and the `dfs-radar` events), foreign networks covering it, the strongest, the pressure, busy (live on a radio's channel, else from the latest survey), noise, and the site's radios on it.
  - *Networks:* every BSS heard, as heard best, with who heard it: the site's own (by BSSID), lookalikes (named like the site's networks but not adopted: waiting for adoption, or an impostor) and neighbors, with the vendor from the OUI, most disruptive first.
  - *Inter-node RSSI matrix:* how each node hears each other node: a live mesh peer link's signal first, else the strongest of the other node's BSSs in the latest scans. It is the link budget behind 11k neighbor reports and the planner (M5.2, M6.1).
- **Events** (observability.md, Events): channel changes (marked when radar came first) and congestion; radar and CAC from the log patterns.
- **API:** `GET v1/rf` (the view: report, each radio's scanning state with its last scan and next time, the settings); WS topic `rf` (the view, then each new one); `POST v1/rf/scans` (admins: `{node?, radio?}`, 202 with the radios queued); `GET v1/rf/scans?node=&radio=&before=&limit=` (stored scans, without what they heard) and `GET v1/rf/scans/{id}`; `PUT v1/rf/settings` (admins: scanning, interval and the planner's DFS policy); `GET v1/rf/plan` (the planner's preview, below). Scans and settings changes are audited.
- **UI:** the band in the URL (`?band=2g`, 5 GHz by default). The spectrum: each network a hill over the frequencies it occupies, as high as it is heard (own in the accent, lookalikes in warning, neighbors muted), contiguous channels in segments with breaks over gaps, DFS channels and radar marked under the axis; hovering a hill or a row selects the network everywhere. The radios as cards (crowding verdict, busy, noise, interference, who shares the channel, last and next scan, a scan button for admins). The channels in use (all on demand), the matrix, each radio's busy share over time (the metric `busy`), and the networks table (all, neighbors, yours). Admins turn background scans on or off, pick the interval and scan everything now.
- Later: scan history views (a network's signal over time) and the "who is on my channel" list per client's AP (M5.2).

## RF planner: auto channel, width and power [M6.1]

The planner is `uniwrt_rf::plan` (M6.1 a1, D-110): pure and deterministic, per band.

**Inputs** (`PlanInputs`): each radio's allowed channels (from its phy and the regulatory domain: DFS flags and state, no-IR, max power), hardware widths, current channel/width/center/power, pins (channel, width, power), whether it carries the mesh, and its clients; the inter-node links per band (the report's matrix, with live mesh links marked); the foreign networks each node hears (primary, span, signal); each 20 MHz channel's survey busy share (not the live share of a radio on it, which counts its own traffic) and radar hits in 30 days; the DFS policy.

**Constraints:**
- **Mesh channel groups.** Radios carrying 802.11s backhaul share one channel and width per band: they are one unit in the search, with the widths all of them support. On dual-band nodes the 5 GHz AP shares that radio, so planning 5 GHz comes down to choosing one channel for the whole mesh group. Where mesh radios are pinned differently, the group follows the first pin (by node), and the others get `pin_conflict`.
- **DFS policy** (D-110): avoid | allow non-weather (not 5600–5650 MHz, channels 120–128) | allow all, **the default**. Channels in radar's non-occupancy period (`unavailable`) are never chosen. The cost counts the DFS risk, more with a history of radar hits, and the CAC's silence when moving onto DFS (ten minutes on weather channels), twice on mesh radios (radar drops the backhaul).
- **Pins:** a manual channel (or width, or power) set on a radio is a hard constraint, kept whatever the DFS policy says.
- **No-IR** channels that aren't DFS (the radio may not start a network there) are left out; two radios of one node on one band never overlap (a cost far above any neighbor's).

**Objective (minimize; every term ≥ 0):**

```
Σ_i L_i · Σ_j w(rssi_ij) · overlap(i ← j)          own: the site's other radios (not its mesh group)
+ Σ_i L_i · (Σ_f w(rssi_if) · overlap(i ← f) + ½ · busy_i)   external: foreign networks, survey busy share
+ Σ_i dfs_i + 0.1 · moved_i + 0.3 · L_i · log2(widest_i / width_i)

w(rssi)        logistic: −90 dBm → 0, −60 dBm → 1 (at the planned powers)
overlap(i ← j) the share of i's channel that j covers
L_i            1 + min(clients, 20) / 10
widest_i       the widest width the unit could take under its constraints
```

Each radio's cost is reported term by term (`own`, `external`, `dfs`, `change`, `width`) before and after; the band's `improvement` is `(before − after) / before`, with the changes counted in `after`. The width term is the width given up (the spec's `−γ · width_benefit`, shifted so every term is non-negative): the planner narrows only where the wider channel costs more than it brings.

**Search:** units of a band start at what runs and descend (one unit at a time to its best choice); bands of up to 10 units then get branch and bound over every combination (`exhaustive`, proven `optimal`; 500k nodes at most); larger ones get simulated annealing (3 rounds, fixed seeds, each from the best so far, then a descent). Ties keep the earlier answer, so equal plans don't move radios, and a plan applied and planned again changes nothing. Each radio gets its reasons (`pinned_channel`, `pinned_width`, `pinned_power`, `mesh_group`, `pin_conflict`, `no_channel`, `narrowed`, `power_lowered`, `power_raised`, `mesh_power`, `below_five_ghz`).

**Channels and widths** (each band's standard blocks, as OpenWrt derives them from a primary channel and `htmode`):
- 2.4 GHz: always 20 MHz, on one non-overlapping set: 1/6/11, or 1/5/9/13 where every radio of the band allows 13 (both are planned and the cheaper kept; ties keep 1/6/11). Sets aren't mixed: partly overlapping channels interfere worse than a shared one.
- 5 GHz: blocks bonded from 36 and from 149; 6 GHz from 1, with both 320 MHz layouts. Widths are **free up to the hardware** (D-110): 160 MHz (320 on Wi-Fi 7 at 6 GHz) where the air is clean, 80/40/20 where it is crowded. In the US only two 80 MHz blocks are free of DFS.
- A block's primary channel is where the nodes hear the fewest foreign primaries, the current one unless another is clearly quieter.

**Power** (before the channel search, which weighs overlaps at the planned powers): each radio whose power is `auto` aims for its loudest neighbor node to hear it at −65 to −72 dBm (enough cell overlap for roaming): lowered when heard louder, raised when nobody hears it above −72 dBm, within 10 dBm and the regulatory maximum, and only in steps of 2 dB or more. A mesh radio stays loud enough for each live peer to hear it at −70 dBm. 2.4 GHz stays 3 dB under its node's 5 GHz radio.

**From the controller** (M6.1 a2, D-111): the engine gathers the inputs on request (`uniwrt_rf::plan::gather`): the enabled 2.4/5/6 GHz radios with their phy's channels and widths and what they run, the mesh radios (an 802.11s interface), clients (stations that aren't mesh peers); pins from a radio override's channel, else the band's when fixed, a radio override's width only, a radio override's power, else the band's; radios of nodes in observe mode (or whose Wi-Fi the site doesn't manage) pinned to what they run (`observed`); links from the report's matrix, foreign and lookalike networks from its networks (once per node, loudest), each channel's radar hits from the report and its busy share from the latest scans' surveys; the DFS policy from the `rf` settings (`dfs`: `avoid` | `non_weather` | `all`, default all).

**Preview** (`GET v1/rf/plan`; `/insights/rf`'s Channel plan card): planned when asked (planning a large site takes a moment), for the band shown: the band's cost before → after, the improvement (marked from 15 %, the nightly threshold), how the search ran, and per radio its setting now and planned, its cost before → after with the terms that count, and its reasons; radios left out are listed; when every radio is in observe mode, it says so (manage a device's Wi-Fi to let the planner move it). Admins set the DFS policy on the card (5 GHz), which plans again.

**Where a plan lives** (M6.1 b1a, D-112): in the site's desired state, `radio.plan` (schema v11): an entry per radio (node, radio, band, channel, width, power) and the mesh's channel and width (`mesh`). Render takes it where the site leaves the choice open: a radio whose channel resolves to Auto (the override's, else the band's) takes the planned channel, unless it can't use it (a warning: plan again; the node's ACS then chooses) or, on a tri-band node, it is the mesh's channel; a radio on its planned channel takes the planned width unless its override fixes one (the band's width yields, as widths are free, D-110); a radio whose power resolves to Auto takes the planned power. The mesh takes the band's fixed channel, else the plan's (on every node, the plan's width with it), else 36 with a warning. Pins always win, so a plan never undoes what the owner set; the planner reads what runs, never the stored plan, so a plan applied and planned again changes nothing. On the Channel plan card admins **Use this plan**: the band's entries are replaced by every radio that isn't observed (observed ones keep what they run), the mesh's channel comes from its group (kept as stored when no mesh radio is planned), and the document is saved as the next revision, reviewed and applied like any other (`/changes`; the review's mesh warnings cover a new mesh channel).

**Apply [M6.1]:**
1. Show a preview, then run a job.
2. Non-mesh radios change one node at a time (M6.1 b1b, D-113): a node whose change is only its radios' channels, widths and powers, with no mesh point on a radio that moves, takes its power with the fleet's first transaction (power alone reloads without a restart) and its channels in the apply's **Channels** step, after the other nodes' changes, one node at a time. Where every radio that moves can announce the switch (an access-point-only radio that is up, on a numbered channel, Wi-Fi 4 to 6: `switch_chan` has no EHT flag; 320 MHz and 2.4 GHz above 20 MHz can't be announced), the transaction installs `wireless` without reloading it (the txn tool's live apply) and hostapd's `switch_chan` (`uniwrt_rf::switch`: the primary's frequency, the block's center, the width, the secondary's side, 10 beacons) moves each radio with its clients; the switch counts once the kernel runs the new primary and width (the agent's `radios` set, polled for up to 10 s). A radio that refuses the switch (a DFS channel whose CAC isn't done) or doesn't get there reloads the node's Wi-Fi instead, and so does a node where some radio can't announce it. Either way the node is verified and confirmed before the next starts; a failure rolls back that node alone (a rollback restarts the switched radios on what they held) and ends the apply partial. netifd and hostapd learn of a switch only when the radio next restarts: its next reload of `network` or `wireless` (a later apply that touches them) restarts it once, on the channel it already runs (D-113; the nightly window, b3, does it on purpose; `network.wireless status`'s `config` shows the channel netifd runs). The review marks such a node (`NodePlan.air`; Impact: "Radio channels move …"). A mesh radio's channel still moves with the ordinary apply, all nodes at once, until b2's synchronized cutover.
3. A mesh group changes in a **synchronized cutover** (M6.1 b2, D-114): where an apply drops mesh links (a mesh point moves to another channel, ID, key or band, or another routing algorithm, leaves or goes off) and more than one node applies, every node of the round reloads at one instant instead of leaves → gateway. The controller sends every node its apply at once, each with the countdown left to that instant, 15 s ahead (`txn apply <id> <s> +<ms>`): the tool snapshots, arms and installs at once, and stops what goes off and reloads only when its countdown ends, on its own uptime clock. No NTP is needed: the error is one command's latency. The dead-man timer counts from the instant. Nothing runs differently before it, so a node refusing its apply rolls the others back before the mesh moved. Verify starts at the instant, with the uplink window (150 s); a mesh that doesn't re-form fails it (each node must hear at least as many batman-adv neighbors as before), and every node rolls back: the controller rolls back the nodes it reaches, and the rest roll back at their deadlines.
4. A nightly optimizer runs in the maintenance window (default 03:30 local). It applies only if the score improves by at least 15% and no heavy traffic is active. This hysteresis prevents churn.

## Coverage & floor plans [M6.2]

- Upload a floor plan per floor, set its scale, and place the nodes.
- The heatmap uses a log-distance path-loss model whose per-band exponent is fitted to the measured inter-node RSSI matrix. Walls can optionally be drawn with materials.
- It shows dead zones and suggests where to add a node. Client positions are not estimated (RSSI trilateration is too noisy).
